# Anti Radar: demo script (2:30)

Read this over `/demo` at a relaxed pace (about 160 words a minute), one paragraph per scene. The timestamps match the trial recording, `demo/anti-radar-demo.mp4`. If you're presenting live, press → when a paragraph ends. Press **H** to hide the bar.

This script was written against the data in the dev database on **20 Oct 2026**. The tour resolves its entities from whatever data is loaded:

- the top formation candidate;
- the strongest verified lab contributor;
- the #1 person this week;
- the largest attention gap.

On another snapshot the names and numbers will differ. Before you present, open `/demo`, read the caption in the presenter bar for each scene, and swap in the names you see there. Every number below was on screen when this was written.

Two things to say if anyone asks:

- **X is disabled.** There is no API token, so social attention shows as "not measured", never as zero.
- **No language model ran.** The meeting brief and the Anti Brief are deterministic templates built from database facts.

**1. Title (0:00).** This is Anti Radar. It isn't another startup database. It looks for technical change before a company becomes obvious. What follows is the real app on real public data.

**2. The radar (0:08).** This is the morning view: what changed, ranked by evidence. Today there are six possible formations, five people worth meeting and three signal collisions. Every row says why it surfaced now and how many sources back it.

**3. Formation Radar (0:20).** Formation Radar looks for people and teams before any announcement. There are ten candidates and all of them are early. Nothing gets promoted without two independent source families. The top one is Kevin Zakka at 51. He has 149 verified commits to DeepMind's MuJoCo Menagerie. In September he started `mjbatch`, which got 600 stars in 40 days.

**4. Signal collision (0:40).** Here the radar holds back. There's one signal, all from GitHub, and no team. The relationships are verified commits, not claims. So far this is talent, not a company.

**5. Why now (0:51).** The timeline gives the dated reason it surfaced: a new public repository on September 10, with its traction, marked FACT and linked to the source.

**6. The person (1:02).** The second thread is Yineng Zhang: 784 verified commits to SGLang, now active in smg-project, a GitHub org about four months old. That history comes from commit records, not his bio. One caveat: our tracked members made only a handful of commits there.

**7. Who should we meet (1:19).** This is the weekly list, ranked by evidence rather than fame. Nobody is high priority this week; Kevin is number one at medium. His meeting brief has 30-second context, what he built, five questions and the things to verify first. Every line is cited and marked FACT or INFERENCE. It's a template, because no model key is set.

**8. Attention gap (1:45).** Tangency Labs is a GitHub org that's four days old. It's shipping a Rust engineering-simulation tool: 105 stars in 30 days and a release two days ago. Technical momentum is 90 against attention 14, and there are no Hacker News threads. X and press weren't measured, and they get no credit.

**9. Anti Brief (2:02).** Investigate turns that evidence into an Anti Brief: why now, founders, why it could matter, why it could be wrong, and questions to ask. Every claim has a source number. It even says that single-source activity is weaker evidence.

**10. Close (2:20).** Find the technical change early, show the evidence, and say what we don't know. Conviction before consensus.
